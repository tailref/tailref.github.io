A free, open-source PureRef alternative

Updated 25 November 2026 Emre Melik Nameli 6 min read

PureRef set the standard for reference image viewers: drop every image you need on one canvas, keep the window above your painting app, and get on with the work. It is small, fast and does exactly one job well.

TailRef Studio starts from that same idea and keeps going. It is a free, open-source reference board for artists that also lets you draw over your references and block a pose with a 3D mannequin — in the same project file, on Windows, macOS and Linux.

This page compares the two honestly, so you can pick the right one for the way you work. TailRef is an independent project and is not affiliated with PureRef.

What PureRef does well

If you only need a pinboard, PureRef is hard to beat. It launches instantly, the download is a few megabytes, and the interface stays out of the way. Dragging images in from a browser, a folder or the clipboard just works, and the always-on-top window means your references sit next to your canvas instead of behind it.

What TailRef adds

TailRef keeps the reference-board workflow and turns the board into a working file for the whole reference stage:

  • Draw over the reference. Open any image on the board in the drawing workspace and trace, paint or annotate it with layers, masks, blend modes and pressure-sensitive brushes. Photoshop .abr libraries import and export.
  • Block the pose in 3D. A two-figure mannequin with IK, camera and lighting sits in the same app. Capture beauty, alpha, depth, normal or wireframe passes up to 8K and send them straight back to the board.
  • Palette extraction. Pull the colours out of one image or a whole selection and keep the swatches next to the reference they came from.
  • Groups, notes, links and locking. Structure a large board instead of scrolling through loose images.
  • Non-destructive finishing. Grade a whole set together, stack effects, and export to PNG, JPEG, WebP, AVIF, BMP, ICO or PDF.
  • Open source. GPL-3.0-or-later, with the full source on GitHub. No account, no cloud, no telemetry.

Where PureRef is still the better choice

PureRef is the lighter tool, and for a pure pinboard that matters. It starts faster, takes a fraction of the disk space and has years of polish behind a single, narrow workflow. If you never draw on your references, never need a pose and want the smallest possible app next to Photoshop, PureRef is a sensible pick — and you can always run both.

TailRef is the better fit when the reference, the annotation and the pose belong to the same project. It is a larger application because it carries a drawing engine and a 3D renderer, and it is younger, so expect rough edges that a mature app does not have.

How to move a PureRef board into TailRef

There is no .pur importer, so a board comes over as images rather than as a file:

  • In PureRef, save or export the images you want to keep, or drag them out of the canvas to a folder.
  • In TailRef, drag the folder or select multiple files onto the board, or paste from the clipboard.
  • Group, crop, rotate and colour-grade them, then save the board as a .tail project so it reopens exactly where you left it.

Frequently asked questions

Is TailRef a good free PureRef alternative?

Yes, if you want more than a pinboard. TailRef does what PureRef does — an infinite reference board, drag and drop, always on top — and adds drawing over references, palette extraction and a 3D posing mannequin. It is free and open source under GPL-3.0-or-later.

Is TailRef really free?

Yes. There is no paid tier, no trial and no account. TailRef is GPL-3.0-or-later open source, and donations are optional.

Does TailRef run on Windows, macOS and Linux?

TailRef ships native builds for Windows 10 and 11, macOS 12 or newer on Apple silicon and Intel, and Linux x86_64 as an AppImage, deb or rpm package. See the download section for the current files.

Can the TailRef board stay on top of other windows?

Yes. The board window can be set to always on top, so you can keep your references over Photoshop, Blender or any other app while you work.

Can I open my PureRef board in TailRef?

PureRef .pur files are not supported. To move a board over, export or drag the images into TailRef, then arrange them on the infinite canvas and save the result as a .tail project.

Can I draw on the reference images in TailRef?

Yes. Any reference opens in the drawing workspace with layers, masks, blend modes and pressure-sensitive brushes, including Photoshop .abr brush libraries.

Is TailRef affiliated with PureRef?

No. TailRef Studio is an independent project by Emre Melik Nameli and is not affiliated with PureRef or Idyllic Pixel. Product names belong to their owners and are used here for comparison only.
